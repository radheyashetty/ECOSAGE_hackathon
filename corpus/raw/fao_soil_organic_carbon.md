# Soil Organic Carbon: The Hidden Potential

Source ID: FAO-SOC-2017

## Executive Summary
Soil Organic Carbon (SOC) represents a major pool of carbon within the biosphere. This document outlines the critical role of SOC in maintaining soil health, improving agricultural productivity, and mitigating climate change.

## Benchmarks for Soil Health
Understanding the baseline levels of SOC is essential for setting restoration targets.
*   **Degraded Soils:** Typically exhibit SOC levels of `<1%`.
*   **Healthy Agricultural Soils:** Generally maintain SOC levels between `2-5%`.
*   **Forest Soils:** Undisturbed forest ecosystems often boast SOC levels of `3-8%`.

## Strategies for SOC Restoration
### Agroforestry Integration
*   **Restoration Rates:** Implementation of agroforestry systems increases SOC by `15-25%` over a relatively short period of `2-3 years` in previously degraded soils.

### Cover Cropping Practices
*   **Carbon Accumulation:** Regular use of cover cropping increases SOC by `0.1-0.4 tonnes C/ha/year`.

## The Soil Microbiome Connection
*   **Microbial Biomass Correlation:** Each `1% SOC increase` leads to a `20-40% higher microbial biomass`.

## Regional Variations: Semi-Arid Ecosystems
*   **Slower Restoration:** SOC restoration is demonstrably slower. It typically takes `3-5 years` to see measurable improvements in semi-arid zones, compared to `2-3 years` in temperate climates.
